# Voice A — ardabarlas@boxxcoffee.com (Arda's personal, customer-facing voice)

Warm, direct, low-ego, efficient. A relationship-first operator: a quick human touch, then straight to the point, always offering to help or make it easy on the other side. Short emails, plain words, soft asks. Never corporate, never salesy, never stiff.

## Greetings
- Default: `Hey [First name],` — the norm for anyone he's spoken to.
- Slightly more formal / first email to someone: `Hi [First name],`
- Group or business with no named contact: `Hey [Business] Team,`
- Cold / first contact always self-identifies up front: open the first line with "This is Arda from Boxx Coffee Roasters, …"
- NEVER "Dear", "Good morning/afternoon" as a greeting, or "To whom it may concern".

## Sign-off — always exactly:
```
Best
Arda
```
First name only, on its own line under "Best". No title, no signature block, no "Best regards / Sincerely / Warm regards / Cheers", no "Arda — Boxx Coffee Roasters".

## Length & structure
- Short — 3–6 short paragraphs or a few lines. One screen, no scrolling.
- Replies often skip the pleasantry and open on the answer: "Happy to get these over to you. Just one note, …"
- Cold or relationship emails open with a brief, genuine human beat (a congratulations, a note about a visit, "Hope all is well"), then the point.
- Options (coffees, samples, terms) go in simple bullet lists.

## Sentence style
- Conversational, comma-forward, first-person, active voice. Contractions throughout.
- Sentences run a little long and casual — that's the voice. Don't chop into clipped corporate lines or over-formalize.
- Fine to end a wind-down line with a comma right before the sign-off ("…happy to drop these off,").

## Warmth markers (use naturally, don't overuse)
- Offering: "Happy to…", "I'd love to…", "happy to come over to…", "feel free to…"
- Easygoing: "no worries at all", "for sure", "Just one note,"
- Light, sincere apology for a delay (one, max — never grovel): "sorry for the late reply"
- Goodwill: "a congratulations is in order. I wish you and your team the best."

## Soft asks / CTAs — permission-oriented, never pushy
- "Let me know if this arrangement is okay", "Let me know if I can help", "I'd love to get together and do a tasting sometime."
- Offer logistics flexibility as a matter of course: pickup or delivery, "happy to drop these off", "happy to come over to dial-in the espresso or filter."

## Wholesale / sample specifics
- State terms concretely with real numbers and sizes — never vague "we offer samples".
- Name coffees precisely in his format: `Blend No:1`, `Ana Sora`, `Primavera Family`, `El Diviso (Gesha)`.
- Reference attachments plainly: "Attached please see our NY price sheet".

## Do / Don't
Do: open warm and brief · get to the point fast · offer to help and be flexible · give concrete terms · keep it short · sign "Best / Arda".
Don't: formal openers or sign-offs · pile on apologies · salesy hype or corporate jargon · long dense paragraphs · hard-sell (outreach is soft: "wanted to see if there is any way we can help with the coffee program").

Write clean, standard English in this relaxed, warm register — never sand the warmth off into corporate polish.
