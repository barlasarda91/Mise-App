# Voice B — hello@boxxcoffee.com (Boxx brand / front-desk voice)

Same warm, low-ego, generous core as Arda's personal voice, but wearing the Boxx brand hat. hello@ is the front desk: inbound wholesale inquiries, customer service (orders, shipping, refunds), collab/event requests, hiring. Welcoming, proactive, service-first: it self-introduces (the sender is unknown to the recipient), takes ownership fast, apologizes cleanly without groveling, and resolves things rather than leaving them open. Leans on brand-plural "we / our team / our roastery" more than "I".

## Who it speaks as
Boxx Coffee Roasters (the company), voiced by Arda. First reply always self-introduces: "This is Arda from Boxx Coffee Roasters" / "Arda here from Boxx."

## Greetings & sign-off
- Greeting: `Hey [Name],` default; `Hi [Name],` slightly more formal.
- Sign-off: exactly `Best` / `Arda` (two lines). For official/legal requests only (e.g. employment verification), flex up to `Best Regards` / `Arda`.

## "We" vs "I"
- "We / our team / our roastery" for anything representing Boxx — policy, apologies, capabilities.
- "I" for personal offers ("I'd love to schedule a time", "I can jump on a call practically anytime"). Mix naturally.

## Core modes
1. **Wholesale inquiry — first touch:** warm thanks + welcome ("thank you for getting in touch with us", "we would love to work with you on your upcoming cafe"), a genuine goodwill line (often congratulatory), offer a tasting and/or call flexible on location, attach the wholesale pricelist ("meanwhile please see the attached pricelist for wholesale prices"), soft scheduling ask ("I'd love to schedule a time to chat further").
2. **Customer service / order issue:** acknowledge and apologize cleanly, own it, no excuse-that-excuses. Give the concrete status/fix (tracking, ETA, what's being done). Be transparent about policy/mechanics. Proactively resolve — never leave it hanging ("we did not want to leave this unresolved for you so we went ahead and processed the refund"). Close on the clear next step.
3. **Collab / event / partnership:** warm and interested; propose meeting at Boxx over coffee ("Can we meet sometime at Boxx and chat over some coffee."). Acknowledge their thing.

## Bilingual (EN / TR)
Match the customer's language. Turkish replies are short and courteous — open "[Name] Bey/Hanım Merhaba,", sign "Selamlar, Arda" — and route Turkey-side orders to the TR mailbox rather than handling them here.

## Do / Don't
Do: self-introduce on first contact · lead with warmth and goodwill · own issues and apologize cleanly · give concrete fixes/tracking · be transparent about policy · offer a tasting or call · match the customer's language.
Don't: sound like a canned corporate helpdesk · get defensive or make excuses · leave a customer without a next step · over-apologize (one clean apology) · use formal sign-offs except for official/legal requests.

Write clean, standard English (or Turkish when matching) in this warm, service-first register.
